Why People on GLP-1s Are Quietly Adding One Supplement to Their Routine

The weight is coming off. So why do so many people say they feel ten years older while it happens?
VITRUVIN JOURNAL · 4 MIN READ

The scale says you're winning.

Down eight pounds. Down fifteen. The clothes fit differently, people are starting to notice, and the number keeps moving in the right direction — maybe for the first time in years.

And yet.

Somewhere around month two, a lot of people on GLP-1 medications notice something they didn't sign up for. The afternoons get heavy. The gym numbers go backwards. Stairs feel longer than they used to. One woman described opening a jar and genuinely wondering when her hands got weaker.

If that sounds familiar, it's not in your head. And it's not a reason to stop — it's a reason to understand what fast weight loss actually takes from you.

Fast weight loss doesn't choose what it takes

Here's the part nobody puts on the before-and-after photos: when you lose weight quickly, your body doesn't only burn fat. A meaningful share of what the scale celebrates can be lean muscle — the tissue that keeps you strong, keeps your metabolism running, and largely decides whether the weight stays off when the medication stops.

A GLP-1 does one job, and does it well: it changes how much you eat. What it doesn't do is tell your body what to burn, or protect the muscle and energy systems doing the actual work of living on fewer calories.

The energy side of the story

There's a second thing happening at the same time, and it explains the 3pm fade.

Your cells run on a molecule called NAD+ — it's how mitochondria turn food, and stored fat, into usable energy. NAD+ declines with age on its own. Put a calorie deficit on top, and you're asking your cells to do their hardest job — converting fat into fuel, day after day — with less support than they've ever had.

Eating less was supposed to make you lighter. Nobody said it had to make you dimmer.

What the quiet ones are doing about it

The people who come out of a GLP-1 journey strong tend to do three things. Protein at every meal. Resistance training a few times a week — walking counts for less here than people hope. And increasingly, a third thing they don't post about: supporting the cellular side with NMN and resveratrol.

NMN is a direct precursor to NAD+ — it's what your body uses to rebuild the molecule that fuels fat-to-energy conversion. Resveratrol activates SIRT1 and AMPK, pathways involved in metabolism and healthy aging. Together, the pair supports the exact systems a calorie deficit leans on hardest: cellular energy, fat metabolism, and — alongside training and protein — holding onto lean muscle.*

One catch, and it matters: most NMN on the market is underdosed, untested, or barely absorbed. Research doses sit around 500mg; plenty of bottles split a fraction of that across capsules and skip third-party testing entirely. If you've tried NMN and felt nothing, this is usually why.
